Where the candle closed

There are two strategies in this repo. One is a neural network that stares at pictures of stock charts. The other is a single line of arithmetic a child could do.

This is the story of how the second one won.

Start here

Every day, a stock leaves four numbers behind

Where it started, the highest it got, the lowest it got, and where it finished. Draw those four numbers and you get a candle. Everything below is built out of candles.

One trading day, one candle

ACT ONE

the expensive idea

Teach a computer to read charts the way a doctor reads an X‑ray

This is a replication of a famous 2023 finance paper. The bet: the same machinery that recognises cats in photographs might recognise about-to-go-up in a price chart. Nobody tells it what to look for. It figures that out from millions of examples.

1

Grab 20 days of candles

One month of trading, for one stock.

2

Draw it as a tiny black-and-white picture

64 pixels tall, 60 wide. No numbers, no labels — just a picture.

3

Show five neural networks millions of these

Five, not one, so a single unlucky model can't run the show.

4

Ask one question: up or down over the next month?

The answer comes back as a probability — 0.51, 0.48, 0.53.

5

Buy the top 10%. Bet against the bottom 10%.

Out of 3,000 stocks, that's 300 on each side.

This is literally what the machine sees

No prices. No ticker. No news. Just white pixels on black — the bars, a moving-average line, and volume along the bottom. Blown up here so you can count the pixels.

And it worked. Beautifully. On the first pass.

THE TWIST

three things were wrong

The number was real. The money wasn't.

1   The winners were penny stocks

The most profitable names turned out to be 30-cent bankruptcy shells. Real on paper, untradeable in life. Putting a $5 minimum price on the universe cut the best cell's return from +23.5% to +11.4%.

2   The arithmetic flattered it

Returns were averaged in logs. A real portfolio doesn't earn that. Redone the way an actual account compounds: +11.3% became +4.2%. Same trades. Honest maths.

3   It wasn't predicting what we thought

Asking “up or down?” quietly taught it to spot which stocks move a lot — not which ones move up. Its skill at forecasting volatility scored 0.097. Its skill at forecasting return: 0.010. Ten times better at the wrong job.

A very expensive volatility detector, deployed as a stock picker.

Worse: a plain decision-tree model, fed 21 hand-written numbers, beat the neural network on the identical task. The pictures weren't earning their keep. After every fix, the honest edge was roughly +4% a year before costs — and about zero after them.

ACT TWO

what survived

One line of arithmetic

Forget the network. Go back to the candle and ask one question: where inside today's range did the stock finish?

clv = ( Close − Low ) ÷ ( High − Low )

The whole signal, on one screen

Why betting against the strong one works

Closing at the top of the range means someone was still buying at the last second — paying up, chasing, out of patience. Closing at the bottom means someone was dumping at any price.

Desperation runs out. Prices snap back.

The strategy doesn't predict the future. It just takes the other side of people who ran out of time. It's the oldest idea in the book: short-term reversal.

the actual recipe

Twelve trades a year. That's it.

1

Take the 500 most-traded US stocks

Nothing under $5 — that's the penny-stock lesson, baked in.

2

On the last day of the month, score all 500

Has this stock been closing higher in its range lately than it used to?

3

Buy the 50 lowest. Short the 50 highest.

Equal dollars in each. Equal money long and short, so the market itself cancels out.

4

Trade at tomorrow morning's opening bell

Never at tonight's close — you can't trade on a price that's already happened.

5

Hold for a month. Then do it again.

Trading it weekly instead was tested: the return collapses from +9.8% to +2.7%.

STILL WRONG

what would break it

Four reasons not to trust this yet

  • 01

    It failed abroad

    The same rule, unchanged, was run in Australia, the UK and Japan. It worked in Australia. It didn't in the other two. Japan was negative. One out of three is a failed test, and it was called a failure in writing before the test ran.

  • 02

    The month-end magic vanished

    On US data the signal is 6.5× stronger at month-end. That pattern replicated nowhere — and in two markets it inverted outright.

  • 03

    Every number here is before costs

    At 8 basis points a trade it still clears. At 25, it doesn't.

  • 04

    The past isn't a track record

    Everything above is a backtest. Real forward evidence starts the day it goes live — and not one day sooner.
